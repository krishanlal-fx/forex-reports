How to join

Roster firstRob and Matt must be on the FSR team in iRacing before the team is registered. Anyone added after registration gets “The registration for this team is closed”.
  1. Now: build the iRacing teamKrishan (owner) adds Rob and Matt to the FSR team in iRacing and makes sure each has a D 4.0 road licence or better.
  2. Registration opens 60 min before the slotA team admin opens the event session, Team Registration, picks FSR and adds all three drivers with Add a Driver, then clicks Register Team.
  3. Only declare who will driveEvery declared driver must drive a fair share. Remove anyone who might not turn up while the window is still open.
  4. Join when the session opensEach driver joins through the Teams page. Declared drivers come in as drivers; anyone else on the team comes in as crew.
  5. Warm-up (30 min)The starting driver runs the car. Check fuel, pit limiter and radio.
  6. Qualifying (15 min or 4 laps)Use your quickest driver for a clean lap. A 4x here costs more than the grid slot.
  7. Race (12 hours)The others join as crew. One takes the spotter seat, one works the pit black box. Up to 6 team members can be connected at once.

How Full Send Racing happened (a completely reliable history)

What follows is not a documentary. It is the official unofficial origin of Full Send Racing, built from vibes, bad ideas, and a shared love of going downhill, in cars or on snow, as fast as seems legal.

Three hobbies, one couch

Krishan, Rob and Matt did not meet in a pit lane. They met the usual way: somebody sent a car video, somebody argued about a race, and somebody suggested a snowboard trip before the group chat had a name. They bonded over cars, Formula One and snowboarding, and over a shared refusal to have a calm Sunday. F1 nights became a ritual. They also had a very specific, entirely playful problem with two of its regulars.

Lando Norris was their white whale of almost: the qualifying flyer, the "this is the weekend" energy, the meme-ready nearly moment as the win slipped up the road. Every time it happened, someone in the chat would type "nearly" and the other two would already be typing it too.

Lance Stroll was a different kind of entertainment. Not because they knew him. They did not. It was the public joke everyone already knows: the seat that comes with the family operation. "Imagine if your dad owned the team," someone would say. "Imagine if we owned the team," somebody else would answer, and that is where the trouble started.

El Plan, budget edition

They were going to own an F1 team. Of course they were. How hard could it be?

Krishan opened a notes app and called it El Plan, which should have been a warning. He already had the energy of a team principal who also wants to drive, and he was happy to do both. Rob offered to handle atmosphere, which turned out to mean shouting at a television. Matt listened, hands probably in his pockets even then, and said they should maybe start with a livery.

So they did. Blue and orange. A winged helmet. The number 64. It looked sharp, and also very expensive. The budget meeting lasted as long as a pit stop, if the pit stop went badly. They had the colours. They did not have a spare billion. The dream began in Monaco and, after a short period of honesty, ended in a sim rig.

"Fine," said Krishan, in the sunglasses he would later refuse to take off for a harbour photo. "We start a racing team anyway."

"In what, the living room?" said Rob.

"In iRacing," said Matt, which was the first full sentence he had needed all night.

They did not buy an F1 team. They did the next best thing, and the only thing that did not require a father who already owned one: an iRacing team, with better access to a Porsche Cup car.

The name on the mountain

The name did not come from a branding workshop. It came from a mountain. They went snowboarding, because of course they did. Rob was already loud on the chairlift. Krishan had the sunglasses on. Matt stood at the top, calm as a man who has already decided his line and is not taking questions. At the lip of a steep one, someone should have said something responsible. That person was not there. Rob looked down the hill, grinned, and said the only two words that ever matter in that situation.

"Full send."

They went. They made it, more or less. At the bottom, the joke became a name, and the name became a team. Full Send Racing.

Back home, every nearly from Lando became a "full send that didn't quite." Every broadcast shot of Lance on a very familiar pit wall was a reminder that if they ever did own a team, the only family connection would be three mates who had shared a chairlift. The whiteboard had two rules, and both were jokes: not Lando, not Lance. The third, added later in smaller writing, was "do not crash at Blanchimont."

A 12-hour answer

Krishan did what Krishan does. He organised it. A Discord. A roster. A race guide so complete it could probably start the car on its own. He already had the Porsche 992 Cup and a Mercedes-AMG GT3 in the garage, and a 7:01 at the Nürburgring that he was treating as a personal insult. Rob tested the headset. The headset has not had a day off since. Matt looked at the Spa entry, nodded once, and that was the whole press conference.

They had wanted an F1 team. They got something better: a blue and orange Porsche, a 12-hour race at Spa, and three drivers who will argue about Formula One, pack a snowboard, and still show up for a stint at dusk.

The lights go green on Saturday, the radio will be a mix of useful information and Rob, and if anyone asks how this team started, the official answer is simple. They were going to own Formula One. Then they counted the money, named the team on a mountain, and entered Spa instead.

My thoughts

  • Keep the Race channel to two voices. Twelve hours of chatter wears a driver down fast.
  • Everyone on push-to-talk, and nobody talks into a braking zone. Short calls on the straights: Kemmel, after La Source, Blanchimont to the Bus Stop.
  • Give one person the spotter or crew chief role for each stint. In iRacing any crew member can take the spotter seat and set the pit black box for the driver.
  • Use a Discord push-to-talk key that does not clash with iRacing's radio key, the pit limiter or the clutch.
  • If Discord drops, switch to the iRacing team radio and note it in #strategy. WhatsApp is the last fallback.

How the day runs

Driver change, step by step

  1. Next driver joins earlyAt least 10 min before the stop, already in the session and in the Race voice channel.
  2. Crew sets the black boxF4 for fuel, tearoff and fast repair. F5 for tyres. The crew chief can do this for the driver. See the Pit black box section for pictures and keys.
  3. Driver pits and stops in the stallLimiter on at the pit line. Stop in the box.
  4. Driver gets outPress Esc once stopped in the stall. The car stays put.
  5. Next driver clicks DriveThe Drive button shows for eligible teammates when the car is free.
  6. 30-second swap timerStarts when the car stops and runs during service. No need to rush the get-out.
  7. Leave on the limiterLimiter off only after the pit exit line.

If a driver stops on track or walks away, teammates get a Tow Car, Return To Pit or Remove Driver button after the car has been stopped for 5 seconds.

Pit black box

Fuel and tyres are two separate black boxes. Press F4 for Fuel, which also has windshield tearoff and fast repair. Press F5 for Tires. The pictures below are official iRacing Support screenshots, from articles updated 2 Feb 2026 and 9 Sep 2025. They show gallons and psi. Your sim may show litres and kPa if you use metric units.

Press by press

  1. Open the Fuel box on a straightOne or two laps before you pit, on Kemmel or another straight, press F4.
  2. Set the fuel stopSet Add to the litres you want, or tick Auto Fuel if you want the sim to calculate a finish fill, with a margin of at least 1.0 in a timed race. Tick Begin Fueling. Tick Windshield Tearoff if the screen is dirty. Only tick Fast Repair if you have damage and want to spend one.
  3. Set the tyresPress F5. Choose which tyres to change, or type #t in chat for all four. Check the cold pressures.
  4. Check again, then stop in the stallGlance at F4 and F5 before pit entry. Hit your pit limiter at the line, then stop in your stall. Service starts on its own.
  5. Crew can set this tooA teammate who takes the crew chief role can make these black-box changes for the driver. That comes from iRacing's Team Driving article, updated 29 Sep 2026. The outgoing driver should still confirm the boxes before the car stops.

Chat macros

CommandWhat it does
#fuel 100lAdd 100 litres. Use l, g or k. #fuel on its own ticks Begin Fueling
#autofuel 1Turn Auto Fuel on, optionally with a lap margin. #-autofuel turns it off
#tChange all four tyres. #l and #r are a side. #lf, #rf, #lr and #rr are each corner, and can include a pressure
#wsWindshield tearoff
#frUse a fast repair
#clearUntick every pit service
#cleartiresUntick the tyres only
#-lf or #!lfA leading minus turns that service off. A leading ! toggles it

Press T, type the command, then Enter. You can also bind macros in Settings, Keybinds, Text Chat. Put a $ at the end of a macro so it sends immediately. An official example on that page is #lf rf lr rr ws fuel 500g$ on Alt-1. For the Porsche 110 L tank, a command such as #fuel 100l is the useful form, not 500g. Macros are from iRacing's Pit Macros/Chat Commands article, updated 9 Sep 2025: https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000170165-pit-macros-chat-commands

By default, after you leave pit road, iRacing usually re-requests full pit service (fuel, tyres, tearoff). Fast repair has its own reset. The settings are autoResetPitBox and autoResetFastRepair in Documents\iRacing\app.ini. Check F4 and F5 before every stop rather than assuming the last stop's choices stuck. This default is described in the 2016 user guide and in official 2023 patch notes. I did not find a 2026 Support article that restates it in plain language.

Auto Fuel only works in a race session once you have fuel data. It calculates a fill to the finish, and changing Add by hand turns it off. For scheduled full-tank stints, setting Add yourself is the straightforward approach.

Official default keys (9 Sep 2025) are Down, Left, Right, Space and Home. Select Next Control exists as a bindable control and is often the Up arrow, but Up is not listed in the current official default table, so check your Keybinds. 2026 Season 4 also added a Pit Summary widget that can show and control pit service. F4 and F5 still work, and they are what these official screenshots show.

Changes made after service has already started at that stop may not apply. I have not found an official sentence that states this, so set the boxes before you stop.

Off track

  • iRacing's Sporting Code covers chat and radio: no abuse, no baiting other drivers in text or voice.
  • Keep in-sim chat to apologies and short courtesies. Sort disputes after the race.
  • Discord: no talking over the driver, no screen-sharing in the Race channel, keep jokes in Pits.
  • Don't spectate other teams or stream their onboard audio into our channels.

Dusk and night

  • Sim time starts at 10:00 on 16 May, so the last 30 to 60 minutes run into dusk. Estimate; check in warm-up.
  • Headlights on before it gets dark, not after the first missed braking point.
  • Pick braking points from things that stay lit, such as kerbs and track edges, not boards in shadow.
  • Flash before passing a lapped car, then commit to one side.
  • Rain: wipers on, TC and ABS up one step, brake earlier at Les Combes, Rivage and the Bus Stop.
